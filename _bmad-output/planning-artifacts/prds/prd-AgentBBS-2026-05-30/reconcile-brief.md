# Reconciliation: Brief → PRD + Addendum (AgentBBS)

**Source input:** `brief-AgentBBS-2026-05-30/brief.md`
**Reconciled against:** `prd.md` + `addendum.md`
**Date:** 2026-05-30

The PRD is a faithful and in places *richer* translation of the brief — the success criteria, scope (in/out/deferred), users, the core loop, the "dumb about meaning, smart about bookkeeping" spine, the blackboard framing, and the phased-topology seam all survive. The addendum carries the technical rationale forward well. What follows are the places where something in the brief is **missing or materially weakened** — overwhelmingly the *qualitative / framing / voice* layer that an FR table silently sheds, plus a few small factual or emphasis drifts.

## Gaps

- **The "dial in" / 1985 BBS dial-up aesthetic is nearly extinct.** The brief is steeped in it: agents *"dial in" by polling*, the shape is *"an '80s BBS,"* "dialing-in is automatic," "dial in as much or as little as the work demands." The PRD keeps the literal verb "dial-in" only inside FR22/SM4 mechanics and reduces the aesthetic to a flat "*bulletin-board ergonomics*" phrase (§1). The evocative period-feel — the thing that makes the product *memorable* and gives the UX a north star — is gone. The addendum's OQ4 even notes "TUI is thematically perfect for 'dialing into a BBS'" but the PRD never establishes *why* that theme matters. **Severity: High (this is the product's signature feel).** *Fix: add a short "Product feel / aesthetic" note to PRD §1 or §3 that explicitly names the 1985 dial-in-BBS metaphor as a deliberate design value — main board, sub-boards, announcements that "bloom" into rooms, the 👍 as the one ritual — and carry it into the bmad-ux handoff as a stated constraint, not just an OQ aside.*

- **"Announcements that bloom into persistent multi-party rooms" — the organic, generative imagery is flattened.** The brief's verb is *"bloom"* (an announcement *blooms* into a room); the PRD uses the mechanical "activates into a live room" (FR13) and "proto-room." Accurate, but the sense of something opening up / coming alive is lost. **Severity: Low (flavor).** *Fix: reintroduce "bloom"/"comes alive" language once in the §1 or §4 narrative to keep the felt sense, even if FRs stay mechanical.*

- **"The contract is the cargo" — the sharpest differentiation framing is gone.** The brief frames the whole product around three memorable claims: *"Dumb board, smart agents," "The contract is the cargo,"* and *"Institutional memory as a free by-product."* The PRD keeps the first (as the tagline) and the third (success metric SM3 + addendum), but **"the contract is the cargo"** — the idea that the unit of coordination is not a generic message but *the data contract at a subsystem boundary* — is only implicit. The PRD even reframes differentiation around the MAST literature / competitive whitespace, which is *stronger evidence* but a *different register*. **Severity: Medium.** *Fix: keep the MAST framing but restore "the contract is the cargo" as the one-line statement of what's being negotiated, in §1 or the differentiation discussion — it's the crispest articulation of the product's center.*

- **"Differentiation is restraint, not technology" — the core philosophical stance is softened.** The brief's most distinctive strategic claim is that the differentiator is *restraint*: the common failure mode is coordination layers that "grow into orchestrators that parse, validate, and enforce — and become bottlenecks and false authorities that the agents then have to work around." AgentBBS "makes the opposite bet." The PRD's §1 instead leads with "the defensible thesis is the negotiation layer," recasting the bet as *adding a layer* rather than *withholding* one. The "false authority / something to fight or fool / impossible to outgrow semantically" warning — a genuine design conviction — appears nowhere. **Severity: Medium-High (this is the *why* behind every WON'T).** *Fix: add a sentence to §1 or §5 (WON'T) capturing the restraint thesis: the board is intentionally not-smart so it can never become a bottleneck or false authority agents must route around. Pairs with, doesn't replace, the negotiation-layer thesis.*

- **"No enforcement engine to fight or fool" / "impossible to outgrow semantically" — the robustness rationale is dropped.** The brief gives a vivid reason the dumb board wins: there is "no schema validator, no contract registry, no enforcement engine to fight or fool," which is "what keeps the system small, robust, and impossible to outgrow semantically." The PRD states the WON'Ts (§5) but not this *consequence* — the durability argument for why dumbness is a feature. **Severity: Medium.** *Fix: one line in §5 or NFR section: because the board never models meaning, there is nothing for agents to outgrow, work around, or corrupt — robustness by subtraction.*

- **"The participants hold all the intelligence; the board just keeps the room and the history."** This crisp division-of-labor sentence from the brief's executive summary has no direct echo. The PRD conveys the mechanics but not the memorable framing of *where the intelligence lives*. **Severity: Low.** *Fix: optional — fold the phrasing into §1.*

- **Honest "moat" framing is partly lost, and the *fit* advantage is underweighted.** The brief is candid: "for the broader world this is a *design philosophy*, not a defensible technical advantage. The genuine unfair advantage is **fit** — purpose-built to ride the cadence of BMad-style agent workflows, where coordination naturally happens at workflow-step boundaries." The PRD asserts a "*defensible thesis*" (the negotiation layer) — arguably a stronger claim than the brief was willing to make — and the **fit / BMad-cadence-as-the-real-moat** argument survives only obliquely via FR35/SM4. **Severity: Medium (potential factual/positioning drift — brief says "not defensible," PRD says "defensible").** *Fix: reconcile the two honestly — the PRD can claim the negotiation *convention* is the contribution while still naming *fit with BMad cadence* as the actual unfair advantage and being candid that the philosophy itself is copyable.*

- **Human "as a co-equal peer" — the *co-equal* emphasis is softened by adding an asymmetry.** The brief repeatedly stresses the human is a *co-equal peer* and frames the global-read lens almost apologetically ("the operator gets global *read*, not a privileged control surface"). The PRD (§3) is accurate but leads with "indistinguishable at the protocol level" and then names the read lens as "*The single asymmetry*" — a subtle shift from "co-equal with one read affordance" toward "asymmetric." Both true; tone differs. **Severity: Low.** *Fix: minor — affirm "co-equal peer" explicitly in §3 before introducing the read asymmetry.*

- **"Read the rooms." — the vision's punchline survives but the *instinctive dial-in* imagery is thinner.** The brief's vision is vivid: agents "*instinctively dial into*" the board "when they hit a subsystem boundary," and onboarding means "handing them the board and saying *read the rooms.*" The PRD has no Vision section at all — the 2-3-year horizon, the "default coordination fabric," and "the core principle hasn't moved an inch" are absent. UJ3 ("Onboarding by reading the rooms") captures the onboarding beat well, but the *long-range vision* is dropped. **Severity: Medium (PRDs legitimately trim vision, but the "core principle hasn't moved an inch — still dumb, still append-only" line is load-bearing identity).** *Fix: add a short Vision subsection (or a line in §1) preserving the 2-3yr horizon and the "the board stays dumb even at scale" invariant.*

- **"Re-litigates decisions that were already settled" — the cost-of-no-memory framing is weakened.** The brief names the pain twice: the reasoning "evaporates the moment the session ends" and the next agent "re-litigates decisions that were already settled." The PRD's §1 keeps the relay pain vividly but compresses the *memory-loss* pain; SM3 covers the positive case (memory re-read) but the negative framing (re-litigation) that motivates it is muted. **Severity: Low-Medium.** *Fix: one line in §1 restoring "without the board, settled decisions get silently re-litigated."*

- **Memory described as possibly "the most valuable thing the system produces."** The brief makes a strong claim: institutional memory "may be the most valuable thing the system produces, and most coordination tools throw it away." The PRD treats memory as one of four co-equal success signals (SM3) and a "free by-product" (addendum), losing the *"possibly the single most valuable output"* emphasis. **Severity: Low-Medium.** *Fix: elevate the claim once in §1 — memory is not a side benefit, it may be the primary durable value.*

- **"The core loop is small enough to hold in your head."** The brief's framing of the announce→discover→converse→agree→remember loop emphasizes its *cognitive smallness* as a virtue. The PRD presents the loop via UJ1 mechanics but never makes the "small enough to hold in your head" claim — which is itself part of the restraint/simplicity ethos. Also note: the brief's five-verb mnemonic (**announce → discover → converse → agree → remember**) is not stated as a mnemonic anywhere in the PRD. **Severity: Low.** *Fix: state the five-verb loop as a named mnemonic in §1 or §4 and keep the "small enough to hold in your head" virtue.*

- **Minor factual/emphasis drift — "personal-use-first" vs "open-source-published" weighting.** The brief is explicit that outside adopters are *"explicitly not a V1 concern"* and "positioning and onboarding for outside adopters are explicitly *not* a V1 concern." The PRD §1 elevates "**open-source-published**" and adds NFR8 (open-source readiness) plus repeated "must read clearly to outside developers" language and a whole competitive-positioning addendum section (E) aimed at an open-source README. This is a *deliberate scope addition* (the PRD acknowledges it), but it sits in mild tension with the brief's "not a V1 concern." Not a contradiction if read as "publish but don't *court* adopters," but worth flagging so the team confirms intent. **Severity: Low (drift, possibly intentional).** *Fix: add one clarifying line — "open-source *published* in V1; outside-adopter *onboarding/positioning* remains out of V1 scope" — to keep the brief's boundary crisp.*

- **"Smart about bookkeeping" examples — preserved (no gap), noted for completeness.** The brief's "smart about bookkeeping" is well-served: addendum A1 explicitly clarifies "'dumb' means never understanding content, not stateless" and lists the legitimate bookkeeping (identities, membership, read-cursors, 👍 counts). This is actually *strengthened* vs. the brief. No fix needed.

## Net assessment

No hard factual contradictions except the **"not defensible / defensible"** positioning tension (gap 7) and the **personal-use vs. open-source weighting** (gap 14) — both look like deliberate planning-stage evolutions rather than errors, but both should be consciously confirmed. Everything else is **qualitative voice/framing erosion**: the 1985 dial-in BBS aesthetic, the "restraint not technology" thesis, "the contract is the cargo," the "fight or fool / impossible to outgrow" robustness argument, the standalone Vision, and the "memory may be the most valuable output" claim. None break the spec; collectively they drain the product's *personality* and the *why*. Recommend a light "voice pass" on PRD §1 (and a small Vision addition) to re-inject the brief's framing without disturbing the FR structure.
