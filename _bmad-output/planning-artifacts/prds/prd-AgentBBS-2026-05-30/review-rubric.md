# PRD Quality Review — AgentBBS

## Overall verdict

This is a strong, unusually self-aware PRD: it has a real thesis ("the defensible thesis is the negotiation layer, not the board substrate"), it earns its differentiation against named competitors via Discovery, and it keeps faith with its load-bearing principle ("dumb about meaning, smart about bookkeeping") consistently across FRs, NFRs, and the addendum. What holds up: strategic coherence, scope honesty, and shape fit are all excellent for a single-operator-but-multi-agent infrastructure tool. What's at risk is Done-ness clarity — several FRs carry the thesis beautifully in prose but lack a testable consequence an engineer could turn into a story acceptance criterion (notably FR30, FR16, FR26, and the SHOULD FR31), and a handful of behavioral edge cases (claim-collision on `register`, `check` cursor semantics under concurrent posts) are asserted rather than specified. For a chain-top PRD feeding story creation, those gaps are the main thing to close before build.

## Decision-readiness — strong

A decision-maker can act on this. The central bet is stated as a bet, not buried: §1 names "The defensible thesis is the negotiation layer, not the board substrate" and explicitly concedes the substrate "is no longer novel," naming five shipping competitors. That is the opposite of smoothing-to-neutral — the PRD volunteers its own weakest flank (substrate commoditization) and argues past it. Trade-offs are named with what was given up: FR2 claim-based auth states the cost ("the board does not authenticate") and the condition under which the decision reverses ("Cryptographic auth arrives with the HTTP backend"). FR23 ("The board never pushes") and the WON'T list ("Push notifications — pull-only") commit to a real sacrifice rather than hedging.

Open Questions are genuinely open and segregated from resolved ones (§10 splits "Still open" OQ1/OQ3/OQ4 from "Resolved during planning" OQ2/OQ5/OQ6), each resolution citing the FR that now carries it. OQ4 even names the live tension ("TUI is thematically perfect... web is more practical") rather than pretending it's settled. The scope-promotion audit (Addendum §F) records four MUST promotions with dated rationale, which is exactly the kind of decision-trail a reviewer pushing back would want.

### Findings
- **low** No `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts anywhere (whole PRD) — The PRD uses `[ASSUMPTION]` heavily and an Open-Questions section, but never the `[NOTE FOR PM]` device at unresolved tensions. The tensions are real (OQ4 form-factor; the CM1 "coordination tax" risk that could invalidate the whole premise). They're handled via OQ/CM instead, which is adequate, so this is informational, not a gap. *Fix:* optionally promote the CM1 net-negative risk to an explicit PM callout, since it's existential to the thesis.

## Substance over theater — strong

Almost nothing here reads as furniture. The competitive/differentiation material (Addendum §E) is the clearest tell: it cites a specific failure taxonomy (MAST, arXiv 2503.13657, "~41.8%... ~36.9%... ~79% combined") and ties the product's one differentiator directly to that number — this is Discovery-surfaced, not template-filled. The Vision (§1) could not swap into another PRD: "a classic blackboard architecture... dressed in bulletin-board ergonomics" and "dumb about meaning, smart about bookkeeping" are product-specific and load-bearing.

Personas are disciplined: exactly two primary participant types plus one explicitly-deferred secondary ("Outside adopters (secondary, not a launch audience)"), and the §3 framing that the two are "indistinguishable at the protocol level" actually drives FR design (FR10 post-by-participation, FR28 global read). No persona theater. NFRs mostly avoid boilerplate by attaching product-specific justification (NFR5 ties polling cost to "well under the relay cost it replaces (guards CM1/CM2)"; NFR9 enumerates four named failure modes and maps each to a guard).

### Findings
- **low** A few NFRs lean on adjectives without thresholds (§8 NFR5, NFR6, NFR8) — NFR5 says cost "stays well under the relay cost it replaces" but names no number or measurement method; NFR6 "small, individually fetchable" has no size bound; NFR8 "documented well enough for an outside developer to stand up the board" has no checkable definition of done. These are product-grounded (not pure boilerplate), so the dimension stays strong, but they recur as Done-ness gaps below. *Fix:* see Done-ness findings.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The PRD has a thesis and the features serve it. The arc: agents negotiate boundary contracts directly (removing the human relay), on a board that is deliberately dumb, with the *only* claimed innovation being the negotiation convention layered on a commoditized substrate. Prioritization follows the thesis rather than ease — the scope-promotion audit (§F) shows `add_participant`, the BMad cadence hook, backup/restore, and the Negotiation Protocol all promoted to MUST *because* they serve the thesis (e.g. the cadence hook "is the delivery mechanism for success signal SM4"), not because they were easy.

Success Metrics validate the thesis rather than measuring activity: SM1 is "zero-relay negotiation," SM3 is "memory that gets re-read" — both are outcome/quality signals, and the PRD pointedly avoids DAU/MAU-style vanity metrics. Counter-metrics are present and pointed (CM1 "Coordination tax... the board is net-negative," CM2 message storms, CM3 stalled negotiations) — and they're the *right* counter-metrics, each naming a way the thesis could fail. MVP scope kind is coherently "problem-solving" (attack the #1 documented failure class) and the MUST/SHOULD/COULD/WON'T split matches that. This does not read as a backlog with headings.

## Done-ness clarity — thin

This is the dimension that needs work before stories. Most FRs are sharp and testable — FR5 ("Project titles are unique on the main board; a duplicate is rejected with a clear error"), FR13 (first reply activates proto-room + auto-joins + seeds message #1), FR20/FR21 (👍 retraction recomputes current contract from the event stream) all have clean, verifiable consequences. But a meaningful minority either describe an outcome no test could anchor on, or assert behavior whose edge case is left unspecified. Because this PRD feeds story creation, each of these will force the story author to invent the acceptance criterion.

### Findings
- **high** FR30 has no testable bound for "stalled" (§6.8 FR30) — "surfaces stalled and active negotiations distinctly enough that the operator can spot a room that never reached a 👍" defines "stalled" only as "never reached a 👍," but a brand-new active room also has no 👍. There's no time-threshold or state distinction an engineer could implement or test against. This is the FR backing the SM3/CM3 oversight loop, so it's load-bearing. *Fix:* define stalled operationally, e.g. "a room with ≥1 message, no live 👍, and no new message in N hours" — or explicitly defer the threshold to bmad-ux and say so.
- **high** FR2 `register`/`login` claim-collision behavior unspecified (§6.1 FR2) — auth is "claim a handle... the board does not authenticate." What happens when a second caller claims an already-registered handle? Silent re-login as that identity? Rejection? Under FR39 auto-derived handles (`amelia-dev@taskflow`) collisions are plausible. The single most security-relevant behavior in V1 has no defined consequence. *Fix:* state the rule — e.g. "claiming an existing handle logs in as it (trusted single-operator model); FR5-style uniqueness does not apply to handles" — and make it testable.
- **medium** FR16 "no catch-up step" is not independently testable (§6.4 FR16) — "sees the full prior history with no catch-up step" restates FR14/FR15 and offers no new verifiable condition; "no catch-up step" is an implementation feeling, not an outcome. *Fix:* fold into FR15 or restate as "a newly added participant's first `read_room` returns all messages with timestamps ≤ join time," which is testable.
- **medium** FR26 seeded-announcement surfacing is unverifiable as written (§6.7 FR26) — "placed so that every agent encounters it" and "surfaced to agents on first `check` / on joining a sub-board" is tagged `[ASSUMPTION]` and is also OQ3 (placement still open). So the one FR that *delivers the differentiator to agents* has no settled, testable trigger. *Fix:* once OQ3 resolves, state the concrete trigger ("the seeded announcement appears in the first `check` result for any identity with an un-advanced cursor"). Until then, flag it explicitly as blocked-on-OQ3 for story creation.
- **medium** FR24 / FR22 `check` cursor semantics under concurrency unspecified (§6.6) — FR24 advances the cursor "on read"; if a message arrives between the query and the cursor advance (NFR3 says multiple processes write concurrently), is it delivered next time or lost? The append-only model (NFR1) makes the safe answer easy, but the FR doesn't state it, and "un-read / replay is out of V1 scope" closes the recovery path. *Fix:* specify the cursor advances to the max event-id actually returned (not "now"), guaranteeing no gap — a one-line testable rule.
- **low** FR31 (SHOULD) inherits all the above with no ACs of its own (§6.8 FR31) — "participate from the UI... using the same shared core" is fine as a SHOULD, but if it ships in V1 fast-follow it needs its own acceptance surface (does UI-posting create a real identity? what handle?). *Fix:* note that UI-participation requires an operator identity/handle and define it when promoted.
- **low** NFR5/NFR6 lack numeric bounds (§8) — "well under the relay cost," "small, individually fetchable" — no thresholds. For an NFR guarding the existential CM1 risk, a target (even a rough one, consistent with the SM "sharpen with usage" stance) would help architecture size the cursor query. *Fix:* add a soft target or an explicit "to be set empirically against the manual-relay baseline (SM2)."

## Scope honesty — strong

Omissions are explicit and do real work. The WON'T list (§5) is principled, not residual: "Contract enforcement / validation / parsing — the board never understands content," "Private rooms / admin god-panel — the operator gets global read, not privileged control." Each WON'T is tied to the thesis, so a reader can't silently assume those features. The MUST/SHOULD/COULD tiers are clear, and COULD items name their trigger ("Networked HTTP daemon... transparent to agents," "Full identity liveness... richer liveness is deferred").

`[ASSUMPTION]` discipline is good and roundtrips: inferences the user didn't directly confirm are tagged inline (FR1, FR3, FR5, FR24, FR26, FR34, FR36, FR38, FR39, NFR3, NFR6) and §10 OQ1 explicitly gathers the identity/project-record assumptions for confirmation "during architecture/UX." De-scoping is done openly via the §F audit and the OQ5/OQ6 resolutions (e.g. "V1 ships minimal last-seen... full liveness/pruning deferred to V2"). Open-items density is appropriate for the stakes: three open OQs plus a bounded set of ASSUMPTIONs on a personal-use-first build is healthy, not a blocker.

### Findings
- **low** No standalone Non-Goals section; WON'T carries that load (§5) — Adequate here because the WON'T list is principled and explicit, so a separate Non-Goals header would be redundant. Noting only because downstream readers sometimes look for the header. *Fix:* none needed; optionally rename "WON'T — out of scope by design" to signal it is the Non-Goals section.

## Downstream usability — adequate

For a chain-top PRD this is the second-most-important dimension, and it mostly delivers. IDs are contiguous and unique (FR1–FR39 with no gaps, SM1–SM4, CM1–CM3, NFR1–NFR9, OQ1–OQ6). Cross-references largely resolve (FR8↔FR9, FR10↔FR13/FR15, FR21↔§6.7↔Appendix A, FR35→SM4). The §7 tool-surface table and Addendum §C field shapes give architecture a clean extraction surface, and §B explicitly addresses bmad-create-architecture while §E/§C feed bmad-ux. Each FR section reads sensibly when pulled out alone.

The gaps are two. First, there is **no Glossary**, and several domain nouns drift in register (see Mechanical notes) — "sub-board" vs. "project" vs. "board," "proto-room" vs. "announcement," "participant" vs. "member." The FRs define these inline (FR6 member, FR10 participant, FR11 proto-room) so meaning is recoverable, but a downstream UX/architecture agent source-extracting terms has to reconstruct the vocabulary rather than look it up. Second, the User Journeys are strong but a couple of cross-refs use "see above"-style anchoring ("Partway through UJ1," UJ2) which is fine inline but means UJ2 doesn't fully stand alone.

### Findings
- **medium** No Glossary for a chain-top PRD with dense, overloaded vocabulary (whole PRD) — "board / main board / sub-board / project," "announcement / proto-room / room," "member / participant," "handle / identity," "current-focus / discovery key" are all load-bearing and used with subtle distinctions across FRs, UJs, and SMs. A bmad-ux or bmad-architecture extraction would benefit from a single authoritative term list. *Fix:* add a Glossary section keying each noun to one definition and the FR that owns it (e.g. member = FR6, participant = FR10, proto-room = FR11).
- **low** UJs anchor on "see above"-style references (§4 UJ2) — "Partway through UJ1, the schema turns out to affect..." means UJ2 can't be lifted out cleanly. Minor for UJs since they're narrative. *Fix:* give UJ2 a one-line standalone setup.

## Shape fit — strong

The PRD is correctly shaped for what it is. Calibration target was "developer/infrastructure tool, single-operator today but multi-agent, chain-top." The PRD reads as a capability spec (§5 Scope, §6 FRs, §7 tool surface, §8 NFRs dominate) — appropriate for an infrastructure tool — while *also* carrying four User Journeys, which is the right call here precisely because the "operator" is multi-agent: the UJs (zero-relay negotiation, third-party pull-in, onboarding-by-reading, operator oversight) each illustrate a multi-actor interaction that the FR list alone wouldn't make vivid, and each has a named protagonist (Rae's API agent, Devi's DB agent, notifications agent, the human operator). That is neither over-formalized (the UJs earn their place by showing agent-to-agent flow) nor under-formalized.

Success Metrics are appropriately operational/outcome-shaped for a single-operator tool (SM2 "I stopped being the message bus") rather than forced into user-facing engagement metrics. The open-source-published stake is handled without over-rotating: NFR8 and the §E competitive material address adoptability without dragging in a full go-to-market the PRD itself flags as out of V1 scope (§3 "their onboarding and positioning are explicitly out of V1 scope"). No shape mismatch.

## Mechanical notes

- **ID continuity:** FR1–FR39 contiguous, no gaps or duplicates. SM1–SM4, CM1–CM3, NFR1–NFR9 clean. OQ numbering is intentionally non-contiguous in display (open: OQ1/OQ3/OQ4; resolved: OQ2/OQ5/OQ6) — every OQ1–OQ6 accounted for, no true gap. Good.
- **Cross-refs:** Spot-checked and resolving — FR8→"NFR/OQ5 resolution," FR21→§6.7/Appendix A, FR35→SM4, FR37→FR1/FR2, FR38→FR2, NFR9→FR30. One soft ref: FR8 cites "NFR/OQ5 resolution" — there is no NFR specifically on liveness; the intent is the OQ5 resolution + FR8 itself. Minor.
- **Glossary:** Absent. Recommended for this chain-top PRD (see Downstream usability). Term drift observed: "board" used for both the whole system and the main board; "sub-board" and "project (sub-board)" used interchangeably; "member" vs "participant" carry a real distinction (FR6 vs FR10) that a glossary should pin.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip:** Inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags present (FR1, FR3, FR5, FR24, FR26, FR34, FR36, FR38, FR39, NFR3, NFR6; Addendum §C "all `[ASSUMPTION]` until architecture ratifies"). There is **no dedicated Assumptions Index** section; OQ1 partially gathers the identity/project-record assumptions but not all inline tags are indexed (e.g. NFR3 SQLite-locking assumption, FR36 default-cadence assumption are not rolled up). Roundtrip is therefore incomplete. *Fix:* add an Assumptions Index, or extend §10 to reference each inline tag.
- **UJ protagonists:** Each UJ has a named protagonist carrying context inline (UJ1 Rae's API agent + Devi's DB agent; UJ2 notifications agent; UJ3 fresh agent; UJ4 human operator). Good. UJ3's protagonist is unnamed ("A fresh agent") — acceptable.
- **Required sections:** Overview, Goals/SMs (+counter-metrics), Users, UJs, Scope, FRs, Tool surface, NFRs, Dependencies, Open Questions, Appendix all present. Addendum cleanly separates downstream-feeding depth. For the agreed stakes and product type, section coverage is complete; the two substantive gaps are the missing Glossary and the missing consolidated Assumptions Index.
